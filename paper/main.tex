\title{\textbf{When AI Exposure Looks Like Trust:\\
Within-Between Decomposition of GenAI Occupational Exposure and
Institutional Trust in Europe, 2012--2024}}
\author{Karla Lučić\thanks{KU~Leuven, Master of Statistics and Data Science. Replication code at \url{https://github.com/karlalucic/MLA}.}}
\date{April 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}\noindent
Using six rounds of the European Social Survey (R6--R11, 2012--2024;
$N=165{,}969$ in 30 countries, 154 country-years) merged with ILO--NASK
2025 GenAI occupational exposure scores \citep{Gmyrek2025},
Eurostat macro indicators, and OECD employment-protection institutions,
I estimate a three-level pooled cross-national multilevel model of
institutional trust. The Mundlak / within--between specification
\citep{BellJones2015,SchmidtCatranFairbrother2016} disentangles the
between-country level effect of mean AI exposure from the
within-country effect of changes in exposure over time. I find a
large positive between-country association ($\gamma_B = +11.24$,
$p=0.002$) but a within-country effect indistinguishable from zero
($\gamma_W = +0.02$); a Wald test rejects equality of the two
($\chi^2(1)=9.66$, $p=0.002$). The cross-sectional positive correlation
between AI exposure and trust does not carry through to within-country
dynamics. A pre-registered education-moderation test
($\gamma_W \times \mathrm{ISCED}$) returns a precise null, falsifying
both the substitution and curator hypotheses for the within-country
margin. Results are robust to country-leverage drops, dropping the
COVID-disrupted R10 round, item-by-item outcomes, leave-one-out
exposure aggregation, and a within-only fixed-effects benchmark.
\end{abstract}
