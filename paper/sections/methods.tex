\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Three-level model.}
Individuals $i$ are nested in country-years $(c,t)$, which are
themselves nested in countries $c$. The empty model is
\begin{equation}
y_{ict} \;=\; \gamma_{000} + u_{0c} + v_{0(c,t)} + e_{ict},
\quad u_{0c}\!\sim\!N(0,\sigma^2_{u_0}),\;
v_{0(c,t)}\!\sim\!N(0,\sigma^2_{v_0}),\;
e_{ict}\!\sim\!N(0,\sigma^2_e).
\label{eq:m0}
\end{equation}
Variance is decomposed into the L3 country share
$\rho_3 = \sigma^2_{u_0}/\sigma^2_{\mathrm{tot}}$ (the intraclass
correlation), the L2 country-year share
$\rho_2 = \sigma^2_{v_0}/\sigma^2_{\mathrm{tot}}$ (the
variance-partition coefficient), and the L1 individual share
$\rho_1$, with $\sigma^2_{\mathrm{tot}} = \sigma^2_{u_0}+\sigma^2_{v_0}+\sigma^2_e$.

\paragraph{Within--between specification.}
The core model M3a augments \eqref{eq:m0} with L1 controls
$X_{ict}$, country-year macro covariates $Z_{ct}$ (round dummies,
GDP growth, unemployment, HICP inflation), and the Mundlak-decomposed
exposure terms:
\begin{multline}
y_{ict} = \gamma_{000} + \boldsymbol{\beta}^\top X_{ict}
+ \boldsymbol{\delta}^\top Z_{ct}
+ \gamma_W \cdot (E_{ct} - \bar E_c)
+ \gamma_B \cdot \bar E_c \\
+ \beta_{\mathrm{genai}} \cdot \mathrm{genai}_{ict}
+ u_{0c} + v_{0(c,t)} + e_{ict}.
\label{eq:m3a}
\end{multline}
The Mundlak Wald is a test of $H_0: \gamma_W = \gamma_B$:
\[
  \mathrm{Wald} \;=\; \frac{(\hat\gamma_W - \hat\gamma_B)^2}
                            {\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\hat\gamma_W) +
                             \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\hat\gamma_B) -
                             2\,\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(\hat\gamma_W,\hat\gamma_B)}
  \;\overset{H_0}{\sim}\; \chi^2(1).
\]
M3b drops the round dummies; the comparison clarifies how much of the
within-effect is global trend versus country-specific deviation.

\paragraph{Random slopes and cross-level interactions.}
M4 adds a random slope on individual exposure
$\mathrm{genai}_{ict}^{\mathrm{gmc}}$, group-mean-centred so the
intercept variance is interpretable at the country mean of exposure
\citep{EndersTofighi2007}. M5 adds the cross-level interaction
$(E_{ct}-\bar E_c)\times \mathrm{eisced}_{ict}$, the H4
adjudication term. M6 restricts to OECD countries and adds the L3
institutional moderator
$\mathrm{EPL}_c \times (E_{ct}-\bar E_c)$ along with the EPL main
effect.

\paragraph{Estimation.}
All models are fit by REML using \texttt{statsmodels.MixedLM} (Python
0.14.6) with the L-BFGS-B optimizer. The 3-level structure is
encoded by passing \texttt{groups="cntry"} for the L3 random
intercept and a variance component
\texttt{vc\_formula = \{"cy": "0 + C(country\_year)"\}} for the L2
random intercept nested in L3. Model comparisons that differ in the
fixed part are flagged for full ML refitting if a likelihood ratio
test is reported; here I rely on Wald tests of single fixed
coefficients and on the variance-component progression.

\paragraph{Inference caveats.}
With 30 L3 units, the asymptotic Wald-normal standard errors on L3
coefficients ($\bar E_c$, $\mathrm{EPL}_c$) are anti-conservative
\citep{McNeishStapleton2016}. The implementation plan calls for
re-estimating these via \texttt{pymer4}'s Kenward-Roger correction;
that step is documented as a follow-up rather than completed here,
and the L3 inferences in Section~\ref{sec:results} should be read
with the qualification that their nominal $p$-values are likely
slightly too small.
