\section{Data and measurement}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Individual data.}
Six rounds of the European Social Survey (R6 to R11, fielded 2012,
2014, 2016, 2018, 2021, and 2023) are pooled at the individual level.
The integrated face-to-face files are downloaded via the ESS Sikt API
(\url{https://api.ess.sikt.no/}) under per-round DOIs (e.g.\
\texttt{ess11e04\_0} for R11 Edition 4.0). The total panel is
$N=276{,}491$ individuals across 36 country codes and 154
country-years (21--30 countries per round). The analysis sample after
listwise deletion of L1 controls and L2 macro covariates is
$N=165{,}969$ in 30 countries.

\paragraph{Outcome.}
The institutional-trust composite is the per-item z-standardised
mean of \texttt{trstprl} (trust in [country] parliament),
\texttt{trstlgl} (trust in legal system), and \texttt{stfdem}
(satisfaction with democracy), with sentinel codes ($>10$) coerced
to missing first. Standardisation is per item across the pooled
panel so that between-country and between-round variance in the
composite is preserved (the multilevel model would have nothing to
decompose otherwise). Robustness models on each item separately
appear in Section~\ref{sec:results-robust}.

\paragraph{Individual GenAI exposure.}
Each respondent's 4-digit ISCO-08 occupation is matched to the
ILO--NASK 2025 occupation-level mean automation score
\citep{Gmyrek2025}, which aggregates 30,000 task-level scores
across 427 occupations. Two vintages are derivable from the 2025
release: the original 2023 GBB scores \citep{Gmyrek2023} and the
2025 ILO--NASK re-scored values, which incorporate GPT-4 / GPT-4o /
Gemini-1.5-era capabilities. I apply a vintage rule:
the 2023 GBB scores for ESS R6--R10 (fielded 2012--2022, the
pre-GPT-4 era) and the 2025 scores for R11 (fielded
2023--2024, the post-GPT-4 era). Within-country variation in
$\bar E_{ct}$ therefore arises from two sources: slow compositional
drift in occupation shares $w_{oct}$, and the sharp
capability-driven shift at R10$\to$R11. A robustness analysis using
the 2025 scores uniformly across all rounds isolates the pure
compositional channel.

The individual exposure measure is z-standardised within the
analysis sample for interpretability and to allow a comparable scale
for the random slope on individual exposure in M4--M6. Coverage of
the ILO--NASK score table is 83.2\% on average across rounds; the
remaining 16.8\% have ISCO codes that are ESS sentinels (66666
refusal, 99999 no answer, 77777 not applicable) or that fall outside
the 427-code ILO--NASK set. These individuals contribute to L1
controls but drop out of the exposure-coefficient sample via
listwise deletion.

\paragraph{Country-year aggregation.}
The country-year exposure $\bar E_{ct}$ is the ESS design-weighted
mean of individual GenAI exposure within each (country, round) cell:
\begin{equation}
\bar E_{ct} \;=\; \frac{\sum_{i \in (c,t)} w_i \cdot E_{o(i),v(t)}}
                       {\sum_{i \in (c,t)} w_i},
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ is \texttt{pspwght} and $v(t)$ is the round-vintage map
above. ESS-internal aggregation at 4-digit ISCO is preferred to a
Eurostat LFS-based aggregation that would coarsen to 1- or 2-digit
ISCO and discard most of the score's granularity. A leave-one-out
variant $\bar E_{ct}^{(-i)}$, which excludes individual $i$ from
the cell average, is used in robustness.

\paragraph{Mundlak decomposition.}
The country-year exposure is decomposed:
\[
  \bar E_{ct} = \underbrace{\bar E_c}_{\text{between}}
              + \underbrace{(E_{ct}-\bar E_c)}_{\text{within}}.
\]
Including both terms separately in the regression instead of
$\bar E_{ct}$ allows the between-coefficient $\gamma_B$ and the
within-coefficient $\gamma_W$ to differ. The Wald test of equality is
the substantive Mundlak test \citep{Mundlak1978}.

\paragraph{Macro and institutional covariates.}
Country-year macro covariates come from Eurostat: real GDP growth
(\texttt{nama\_10\_gdp}), unemployment rate (\texttt{une\_rt\_a},
ages 15--74, both sexes), and HICP inflation
(\texttt{prc\_hicp\_aind}). Each ESS round is paired with its modal
fielding year (R6$\to$2012, R7$\to$2014, \ldots, R10$\to$2021,
R11$\to$2023). Israel and Russia are not in Eurostat and so drop
from the macro-controlled models via listwise deletion (4.9\% of
the panel). The L3 institutional moderator is the OECD Employment
Protection Legislation index, version 1, regular contracts, 2007
baseline (pre-period for exogeneity); 23 of the 36 ESS countries
are OECD members and have a non-missing value. Welfare-regime
typology follows the Esping-Andersen tradition with a Western Balkans
extension to the Eastern-European class.
