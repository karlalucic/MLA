\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the within--between gap means.}
The headline gap is the canonical
\citet{SchmidtCatranFairbrother2016} configuration in which a
specification that constrains $\gamma_W = \gamma_B$ would mis-estimate
both. Read substantively, the cross-sectional positive correlation
reflects the characteristics of high-exposure countries---income per
capita, service-economy share, institutional capacity---and not AI
exposure itself. Within a given country over 2012--2024, the variation
in country-mean exposure that is identifiable separately from a global
time trend is too small, or too weakly related to trust, to move the
outcome detectably.

\paragraph{Why this is not the usual dislocation story.}
The single-country economic-dislocation literature
\citep{ColantoneStanig2018,FreyBergerChen2018,AnelliColantoneStanig2021}
identifies effects from trade or technology shocks within a shared
national institutional environment, where between-region variation in
exposure is more plausibly separable from baseline trust. Pooling
cross-nationally changes the estimand. Across countries, exposure also
marks prosperity, sectoral structure, and institutional capacity, so
the same coefficient is much easier to confound. The literature's
mechanism (structural grievance, performance disappointment) may still
hold locally within countries; this paper does not rule it out. It
shows that, at the European multi-country scale, the within--between
distinction reshapes the answer.

\paragraph{Planned null on H4.}
The H4 cross-level interaction was specified with both substitution and
curator readings plus a precise null, and the data return the null. The
within-country exposure effect---such as it is---is not heterogeneous
in the individual education gradient. This is informative rather than
inconclusive: the interaction coefficient is small in absolute value
and reasonably precise, not an imprecise estimate with the wrong sign.

\paragraph{Endogeneity.}
The within--between specification handles time-invariant country-level
confounders---welfare-state arrangements, baseline political culture,
geography---but not time-varying ones. A confounder that moves with
country-year exposure and trust in the same direction (contemporaneous
shifts in media environment, immigration policy, or party-system
fragmentation) would not be absorbed by the country random intercept.
Reverse causation is also plausible: declining trust may accelerate AI
adoption via labour-market deregulation or reduced public investment in
retraining. The cross-sectional estimate $\hat\gamma_B$ should be read
as an association \emph{within a multilevel structure}, not a causal
effect.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Four limitations matter beyond the endogeneity caveat. \emph{First},
the L2 country-year VPC of $0.025$ is at the soft floor where a
within--between decomposition is well-powered to distinguish $\gamma_W$
from $\gamma_B$; the Wald rejection of equality is therefore driven by
$\hat\gamma_B$'s magnitude and $\hat\gamma_W$'s precision rather than
by strong identifying signal in $\hat\gamma_W$ itself. \emph{Second},
six countries drop from M3a on Eurostat coverage gaps
(Section~\ref{sec:data}); patches from non-Eurostat sources are avoided
to preserve cross-country comparability. \emph{Third}, leave-one-out
aggregation yields $\hat\gamma_B = +10.43$, only modestly below the
headline $+11.24$, so the mechanical correlation between own and cell
exposure inflates $\hat\gamma_B$ slightly but not substantially.
\emph{Fourth}, three avenues are outside the submitted pipeline:
alternative occupation-exposure measures
\citep{Webb2020,Felten2021,Felten2023,Eloundou2024} that require an
ISCO-08$\leftrightarrow$SOC-2010 crosswalk; the Kenward--Roger
correction via \texttt{pymer4}/\texttt{lmerTest}; and multiple
imputation of \texttt{hinctnta}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The diffusion of generative AI capabilities since 2022 has not, in the
period this panel covers, moved European institutional trust within
countries in the way single-country dislocation studies might lead one
to expect. The cross-sectional positive association between AI exposure
and trust does not reappear as a within-country effect once the two
margins are separated. Whether this changes as the GenAI capability
frontier diffuses further into white-collar labour markets is an open
empirical question that the next ESS round (R12, fielding 2026) will
let us begin to answer.
